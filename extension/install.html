Map your own followers

See who among your followers know each other, with your friend groups as coloured clusters. Setting it up takes about three minutes. Then it maps your followers on its own, which takes about an hour for every 500.

This part needs a computer

The mapping runs in Chrome on a Mac or a Windows computer. Send this page to yourself and open it there. The graph you get works on your phone too.

Open this page in Chrome

The extension runs in Chrome, and also in Edge, Brave and Arc. Copy this page's link and paste it in one of those. If you don't have any, get Chrome first.

  1. Download the extension

    Download IG Social Graph

    Open your Downloads and double-click ig-social-graph.zip. That gives you a folder called ig-social-graph.

    Move that folder to Documents. Chrome runs the extension from it, so it has to stay where Chrome can find it.

  2. Open Chrome's extensions page

    Chrome only opens this page from its own address bar. Copy the address, paste it in a new tab and press Enter.

    chrome://extensions

  3. Turn on Developer mode

    It's the switch in the top-right corner of that page. It lets Chrome add an extension from a folder on your computer.

    Extensions
    Search extensions Developer mode

  4. Click “Load unpacked” and pick the folder

    A new row of buttons appears. Click Load unpacked, choose the ig-social-graph folder in Documents and click Select.

    Load unpacked Pack extension Update

  5. Follow the welcome tab

    A welcome tab opens by itself. It shows you how to keep the extension's icon in view, checks that you're logged in to Instagram, and has the button that starts the mapping.

What it does with your account

  • It reads your followers lists from your own logged-in Instagram tab, one page every six seconds, the way you would scroll them.
  • It never posts, follows, likes or messages anyone, and it never sees your password.
  • What it collects stays in your browser. It leaves only when you press Copy share link.
  • A share link is public: anyone who has it sees the graph, with names and photos.
  • If Instagram asks it to slow down, it waits and carries on by itself.

If something looks different

There is no “Load unpacked” button

Developer mode is still off. Turn on the switch in the top-right corner of the extensions page.

Chrome says “Manifest file is missing or unreadable”

The folder you picked is one level too high, or it's the zip itself. Open the folder you picked: the right one has a file called manifest.json directly inside it.

Chrome asks to “Disable developer mode extensions”

Some versions of Chrome ask this when they start. Close the message with ✕, and the extension keeps working.

The extension disappeared

The folder was moved or deleted. Put it back in Documents and do step 4 again. What it collected is kept.
